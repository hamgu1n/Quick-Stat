## Section 3.4 – Summary Statistics

By now we have explored the center of a dataset through the mean, median, and mode, and the spread through the range, variance, standard deviation, and IQR. In practice, we rarely report just one of these values in isolation. Instead, we combine them into a concise summary that gives a balanced picture of the data. This section introduces the **five-number summary** and discusses how center and spread work together to describe a distribution.

### The Five-Number Summary

The **five-number summary** is exactly what it sounds like: five values that together capture the overall shape of a dataset. They are:

1. The **minimum**, the smallest observation
2. The **first quartile** ($Q_1$), 25th percentile
3. The **median** ($Q_2$), 50th percentile
4. The **third quartile** ($Q_3$), 75th percentile
5. The **maximum**, the largest observation

Together, these five numbers tell us where the data starts and ends, where the middle half lives, and where the center falls. As we will see in Chapter 4, the five-number summary is the foundation of the **boxplot**, one of the most useful graphical tools in descriptive statistics.

**Example 3.4.1 – Computing the Five-Number Summary**

The following are the heights (in inches) of 11 players on a basketball team:

$$
66, 68, 70, 71, 72, 74, 75, 76, 78, 80, 84
$$

Find the five-number summary.

<Solution>

The data are already sorted, with $n = 11$.

- **Minimum** = 66
- **Median** = 6th value = 74
- **$Q_1$** = median of the lower half ($66, 68, 70, 71, 72$) = 70
- **$Q_3$** = median of the upper half ($75, 76, 78, 80, 84$) = 78
- **Maximum** = 84

Five-number summary: **66, 70, 74, 78, 84**.

</Solution>
</div>

<div className="example">
**Example 3.4.2 – Reading a Five-Number Summary**

A researcher reports the following five-number summary for the daily number of steps walked by a group of office workers:

- Minimum: 1,200
- $Q_1$: 4,500
- Median: 7,100
- $Q_3$: 9,800
- Maximum: 18,400

What does this tell us about the distribution? Is the data symmetric or skewed?

<Solution>

- The middle 50% of workers walk between 4,500 and 9,800 steps, so IQR = 5,300 steps.
- The median (7,100) sits close to the center of the box: it is 2,600 above $Q_1$ and 2,700 below $Q_3$.
- However, the upper tail is much longer than the lower one. The maximum is 8,600 above $Q_3$, while the minimum is only 3,300 below $Q_1$.

The middle of the data is fairly symmetric, but the long upper tail suggests the distribution is **skewed right**, with a few workers walking far more than everyone else.

</Solution>
</div>

### Combining Center and Spread

Reporting only a measure of center can be deeply misleading. Consider two classes that both have a mean exam score of 75. In the first class, every student scored between 70 and 80, the scores are tightly packed. In the second class, scores ranged from 30 to 100, wildly spread out. The mean alone cannot distinguish these two situations; you also need the spread.

The most common pairings in practice are:
- **Mean** with **standard deviation**: best for symmetric distributions without strong outliers
- **Median** with **IQR**: best when the data are skewed or when outliers are present

When a distribution is **symmetric** (roughly mirror-image on both sides), the mean and median will be close to each other, and the standard deviation provides a natural description of spread. When a distribution is **skewed**, pulled in one direction by a long tail, the mean is dragged toward that tail while the median stays closer to the bulk of the data. In that case, the median and IQR paint a more honest picture.

<div className="example">
**Example 3.4.3 – Choosing the Right Pair**

For each dataset, decide whether you would report the mean + standard deviation or the median + IQR, and explain why.

1. The salaries of 200 employees at a tech company, including a handful of executives earning several million dollars per year.
2. The quiz scores of a class of 25 students on a 10-point quiz, where most students scored between 7 and 9.
3. The number of hours per week spent on social media by college students, with a few students reporting 60+ hours per week.

<Solution>

1. **Median + IQR.** The executives' salaries are extreme outliers that would pull the mean upward and inflate the standard deviation.
2. **Mean + standard deviation.** The scores are tightly clustered with no extreme values, so the distribution is roughly symmetric.
3. **Median + IQR.** The few students reporting 60+ hours create a long right tail that would distort the mean and standard deviation.

</Solution>
</div>

<div className="example">
**Example 3.4.4 – Symmetric vs. Skewed**

Dataset A has the following five-number summary: Min = 40, $Q_1$ = 55, Median = 60, $Q_3$ = 65, Max = 80. The mean is 59.

Dataset B has: Min = 10, $Q_1$ = 55, Median = 62, $Q_3$ = 68, Max = 200. The mean is 74.

For each dataset, identify whether the distribution appears symmetric or skewed, and explain which pair of summary statistics you would report.

<Solution>

**Dataset A** is **roughly symmetric**. The median (60) is in the middle of the box (5 from each quartile), the whiskers are equal length (15 on each side), and the mean (59) is close to the median. Report the **mean and standard deviation**.

**Dataset B** is **skewed right**. The maximum (200) is far above $Q_3$, and the mean (74) is pulled well above the median (62) by the high values. (The low minimum of 10 suggests a low outlier as well.) Report the **median and IQR**, since they are resistant to the extreme values.

</Solution>
</div>

<div className="example">
**Example 3.4.5 – Full Summary Report**

Below are the monthly electric bills (in dollars) for 12 households in a neighborhood:

$$
85, 90, 92, 95, 98, 100, 105, 108, 110, 115, 120, 210
$$

1. Calculate the mean and standard deviation.
2. Find the five-number summary.
3. Does an outlier appear to be present? If so, which measure of center and spread would you prefer to report?

<Solution>

1. **Mean and standard deviation:**

$$
\bar{x} = \frac{1328}{12} \approx 110.67 \text{ dollars} \qquad s \approx 32.98 \text{ dollars}
$$

2. **Five-number summary** ($n = 12$, so each half has 6 values):
   - Min = 85
   - $Q_1 = (92 + 95)/2 = 93.5$
   - Median $= (100 + 105)/2 = 102.5$
   - $Q_3 = (110 + 115)/2 = 112.5$
   - Max = 210

3. **Yes, 210 is an outlier.** IQR $= 112.5 - 93.5 = 19$, so the upper fence is $112.5 + 1.5(19) = 141$, and $210 > 141$. Because of this outlier, the **median and IQR** are the better summary. Notice how much the outlier inflates the standard deviation: without 210, $s$ drops from about 33 to about 11.

</Solution>
</div>

### Key Points – Summary Statistics
- The five-number summary (minimum, $Q_1$, median, $Q_3$, maximum) provides a compact description of a dataset's shape
- Mean and standard deviation work best together for symmetric distributions without strong outliers
- Median and IQR work best together for skewed distributions or data with outliers
- A skewed distribution has a long tail in one direction; the mean is pulled toward that tail while the median stays near the center
- Always report both a measure of center and a measure of spread, center alone is never sufficient
