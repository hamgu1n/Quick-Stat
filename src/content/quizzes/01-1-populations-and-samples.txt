### A researcher wants to know how many hours per week Roanoke College students spend studying. She surveys 120 Roanoke students. What is the population?
@ section-1-1-populations-and-samples
- The 120 students she surveyed
- All college students in the United States
+ All Roanoke College students
- The number of hours each student studies
> The population is the full group we want to draw conclusions about: every Roanoke student. The 120 students surveyed are the sample.

### Why do statisticians usually study a sample instead of the whole population?
@ section-1-1-populations-and-samples
- Samples always give the exact same answer as the population
- Populations are too small to be useful
+ It is usually impossible or impractical to examine every member of a large population
- A sample removes the need to think about bias
> Populations can contain thousands or millions of individuals, so we examine a smaller portion and use it to draw conclusions about the whole.

### What makes a sample biased?
@ section-1-2-biased-sampling
- It contains fewer than 30 people
- It was chosen using random numbers
- It includes people with different opinions
+ One part of the population is favored or weighted more than others
> A biased sample over-represents some portion of the population, so it doesn't reflect the population as a whole.

### A reporter interviews the first 15 people who walk out of a gym about their exercise habits. What kind of sample is this?
@ section-1-2-biased-sampling
- Simple random sample
+ Convenience sample
- Systematic sample
- Cluster sample
> The reporter picks the people who are easiest to reach. Gym-goers probably exercise more than the general public, so the sample is biased.

### A website asks visitors to rate a new movie, and only people who choose to respond are counted. Why is this likely to be biased?
@ section-1-2-biased-sampling
- The sample size is always too large
- Every visitor has an equal chance of being included
+ People with strong opinions are more likely to respond than people who feel neutral
- Online surveys can't record numbers accurately
> This is a voluntary response sample. People choose themselves, so extreme opinions tend to be over-represented.

### A teacher puts all 30 students' names in a hat, mixes them well, and draws 5. What type of sample is this?
@ section-1-3-unbiased-sampling
- Stratified sample
- Systematic sample
- Convenience sample
+ Simple random sample
> Every student, and every group of 5 students, has the same chance of being chosen. That defines a simple random sample.

### A store manager surveys every 8th customer who walks in, starting with a randomly chosen customer among the first 8. What type of sample is this?
@ section-1-3-unbiased-sampling
+ Systematic sample
- Cluster sample
- Simple random sample
- Voluntary response sample
> A systematic sample follows a fixed rule (every 8th customer) with a random starting point.

### In stratified sampling, what are the **strata**?
@ section-1-3-unbiased-sampling
- The individuals who refuse to take part
- The randomly selected groups where everyone is surveyed
- The list of everyone in the population
+ Groups that share a characteristic, such as grade level, from each of which we randomly sample
> The population is divided into strata (like freshmen, sophomores, juniors, and seniors), and a random sample is taken from every stratum.

### A city randomly selects 4 of its 40 elementary schools and surveys every student in those 4 schools. What type of sample is this?
@ section-1-3-unbiased-sampling
+ Cluster sample
- Stratified sample
- Systematic sample
- Convenience sample
> Whole groups (schools) are chosen at random and everyone in them is surveyed. In a stratified sample, we would sample some students from every school instead.

### What is the key difference between stratified sampling and cluster sampling?
@ section-1-3-unbiased-sampling
- Stratified sampling is biased and cluster sampling is unbiased
+ Stratified sampling randomly picks individuals from every group; cluster sampling randomly picks whole groups
- Cluster sampling never uses randomness
- There is no difference; the terms mean the same thing
> Both divide the population into groups. Stratified sampling samples within every group, while cluster sampling selects entire groups at random.
