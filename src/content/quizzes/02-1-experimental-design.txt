Section Review: Experimental Design. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### What makes a study an experimental study?
@ section-1-4-observational-vs-experimental-studies
- Researchers record what already happens without changing anything
- The study uses a very large sample
- The data are collected with a survey
+ Researchers deliberately assign a treatment and measure the response
> In an experiment, the researcher decides who gets which treatment. In an observational study, they only observe and record.

### Researchers track the coffee intake and blood pressure of 500 adults for a year without telling them what to drink. What kind of study is this?
@ section-1-4-observational-vs-experimental-studies
- Experimental study
- Double-blind experiment
+ Observational study
- Cluster sample
> No treatment is assigned; the researchers simply record what people already do.

### An observational study finds that students who sleep more tend to earn higher grades. What can we conclude?
@ section-1-4-observational-vs-experimental-studies
- Sleeping more causes higher grades
+ Sleep and grades are associated, but this study can't show cause and effect
- Higher grades cause students to sleep more
- There is no relationship between sleep and grades
> Observational studies show associations. Only a well-designed experiment can establish cause and effect.

### Why might researchers choose an observational study instead of an experiment?
@ section-1-4-observational-vs-experimental-studies
- Observational studies can prove causation more easily
+ Assigning the treatment would be unethical or impractical
- Observational studies don't need any data
- Experiments can't use random samples
> For example, we can't ethically force students to go without sleep, so we observe their existing habits instead.

### What is the main purpose of randomly assigning subjects to treatment groups?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
- To make sure the groups have different sizes
- To let participants pick the treatment they prefer
- To guarantee that the treatment works
+ To balance unknown variables across groups and avoid hidden bias
> Random assignment spreads differences among subjects (known and unknown) evenly across the groups, so they don't bias the results.

### In a drug trial, one group gets the drug and another gets a placebo. What is the placebo group called?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
- The treatment group
- The blinded group
- The confounding group
+ The control group
> The control group doesn't receive the treatment, giving a baseline to compare against.

### In a study, the participants don't know whether they got the real drug, but the doctors measuring their health do know. What type of study is this?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
+ Single-blind
- Double-blind
- Unblinded
- Observational
> Only the participants are unaware of their treatment, so it is single-blind. In a double-blind study, the researchers wouldn't know either.

### Why is double-blinding useful?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
+ It keeps both participants' and researchers' expectations from influencing the results
- It makes the sample size larger
- It replaces the need for a control group
- It guarantees random assignment
> If either side knows who got the treatment, expectations can change how people feel, respond, or are measured.

### The first 25 patients to arrive get a new drug and the next 25 get a placebo. Which principle is violated?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
- Control
+ Randomization
- Blinding
- None; this is a well-designed experiment
> Treatment depends on arrival time instead of chance. Early arrivals might differ in a systematic way, such as being in more pain.

### What is a confounding factor?
@ section-1-5-randomization-control-and-blinding
+ An outside variable that can distort the results if not accounted for
- The treatment the researcher is testing
- The response being measured
- A participant who drops out of the study
> Randomization, control, and blinding all work to reduce confounding, so differences can be traced to the treatment.
