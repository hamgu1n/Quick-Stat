### Why do we need measures of spread in addition to measures of center?
@ section-2-2-measures-of-dispersion
+ Two datasets can share the same center but differ a lot in variability
- Measures of center are always wrong
- Spread tells us the most common value
- Spread replaces the need for the mean
> The center alone doesn't tell us how spread out the values are, so we report both.

### What is the range of $6, 14, 3, 21, 9$?
@ range
- 15
- 21
- 9
+ 18
> Range = Maximum − Minimum = $21 - 3 = 18$.

### What is the main weakness of the range?
@ range
- It is too hard to calculate
+ It depends on only two values, so one outlier can change it drastically
- It can be negative
- It is measured in squared units
> The range uses only the minimum and maximum, so it ignores the rest of the data.

### What is the sample variance of $2, 4, 6$?
@ variance-and-standard-deviation
- 2.67
- 8
+ 4
- 2
> $\bar{x} = 4$. Squared deviations: $4, 0, 4$, which sum to 8. Divide by $n - 1 = 2$: $s^2 = 4$.

### In the sample variance formula, what do we divide by?
@ variance-and-standard-deviation
- $n$
- $n + 1$
- $\bar{x}$
+ $n - 1$
> $s^2 = \frac{1}{n-1}\sum (x_i - \bar{x})^2$.

### A sample has variance $s^2 = 25$. What is the standard deviation?
@ variance-and-standard-deviation
- 625
+ 5
- 12.5
- 25
> The standard deviation is the square root of the variance: $\sqrt{25} = 5$.

### Why is the standard deviation often easier to interpret than the variance?
@ variance-and-standard-deviation
- It is always smaller than the variance
- It ignores outliers
+ It is in the same units as the original data
- It only uses the middle 50% of the data
> Variance is in squared units (like hours²). Taking the square root brings it back to the data's units.

### Dataset A has $s = 2$ and dataset B has $s = 10$. Which statement is true?
@ variance-and-standard-deviation
- Dataset A has a larger mean
- Dataset A has more outliers
+ Dataset B's values are more spread out around its mean
- The datasets must have the same range
> A larger standard deviation means the data are more spread out; a smaller one means they're tightly clustered.

### What does the IQR measure?
@ interquartile-range-iqr
+ The spread of the middle 50% of the data
- The spread of all the data, from minimum to maximum
- The average distance from the mean
- The most common value
> $\text{IQR} = Q_3 - Q_1$, the width of the middle half of the data.

### For a dataset with $Q_1 = 12$ and $Q_3 = 30$, what is the IQR, and is it robust to outliers?
@ interquartile-range-iqr
- 42; no
+ 18; yes
- 18; no
- 21; yes
> $\text{IQR} = 30 - 12 = 18$. It ignores the lowest and highest 25% of the data, so outliers don't affect it much.
