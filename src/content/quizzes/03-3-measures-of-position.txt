### Your exam score is at the 85th percentile. What does that mean?
@ percentiles-and-quartiles
+ You scored at or above about 85% of the students who took the exam
- You answered 85% of the questions correctly
- You scored 85 points
- 85% of students scored higher than you
> Percentiles describe relative standing: the percentage of observations at or below your value. They say nothing about the percentage of questions answered correctly.

### Which quartile is the same as the median?
@ percentiles-and-quartiles
- $Q_1$
- $Q_3$
- None of them
+ $Q_2$
> $Q_2$ is the 50th percentile, which is the median.

### For the sorted data $3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18, 21$, what is $Q_1$?
@ percentiles-and-quartiles
- 5
- 7
+ 6
- 8
> The median is the 5th value, 12. The lower half is $3, 5, 7, 8$, whose median is $(5 + 7)/2 = 6$.

### For the same data, $3, 5, 7, 8, 12, 13, 14, 18, 21$, what is $Q_3$?
@ percentiles-and-quartiles
+ 16
- 14
- 18
- 13.5
> The upper half is $13, 14, 18, 21$, whose median is $(14 + 18)/2 = 16$.

### A student scores 90 on a test with mean 78 and standard deviation 6. What is the z-score?
@ z-scores
- 1.5
- 12
+ 2
- −2
> $z = (90 - 78)/6 = 12/6 = 2$. The score is 2 standard deviations above the mean.

### A value has a z-score of $-1.5$. What does that tell you?
@ z-scores
- The value is 1.5 units below the mean
+ The value is 1.5 standard deviations below the mean
- The value is 1.5 standard deviations above the mean
- The value is an outlier
> The sign gives the direction (negative means below the mean), and the size counts standard deviations.

### Student A scores 85 in a class with mean 75 and SD 5. Student B scores 90 in a class with mean 82 and SD 8. Who did better relative to their class?
@ z-scores
- Student B
- They did equally well
- We can't compare different classes
+ Student A
> $z_A = (85 - 75)/5 = 2$ and $z_B = (90 - 82)/8 = 1$. Student A is further above their class mean.

### A dataset has $Q_1 = 20$ and $Q_3 = 30$. What is the upper fence for the IQR outlier rule?
@ outlier-detection-using-the-iqr-rule
- 40
+ 45
- 35
- 60
> $\text{IQR} = 10$, so the upper fence is $Q_3 + 1.5 \times \text{IQR} = 30 + 15 = 45$.

### With $Q_1 = 20$ and $Q_3 = 30$, which of these values would be flagged as a potential outlier?
@ outlier-detection-using-the-iqr-rule
- 8
- 36
- 44
+ 3
> The fences are $20 - 15 = 5$ and $30 + 15 = 45$. Only 3 falls outside them.

### The IQR rule flags a value as a potential outlier. What should you do?
@ outlier-detection-using-the-iqr-rule
- Delete it immediately
+ Investigate it: it may be an error or a genuinely unusual observation
- Replace it with the mean
- Ignore the rule, since it is rarely correct
> The rule gives an objective starting point, but context matters. A flagged value shouldn't be removed automatically.
