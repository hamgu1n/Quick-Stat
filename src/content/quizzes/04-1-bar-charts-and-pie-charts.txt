Section Review: Bar Charts and Pie Charts. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### What type of data are bar charts and pie charts designed for?
@ section-2-5-bar-charts-and-pie-charts
- Numerical (quantitative) data
- Paired numerical data
- Only data with outliers
+ Categorical (qualitative) data
> Both charts show how observations are split among categories.

### Why don't the bars in a bar chart touch?
@ bar-charts
+ To show that the categories are separate, not a continuous scale
- To save space on the page
- Because the bars represent percentages
- Because each bar has a different width
> The gaps reinforce that the categories are distinct. Histograms, which show numerical data, have touching bars.

### In a survey of 80 students, 20 chose basketball as their favorite sport. What is basketball's relative frequency?
@ bar-charts
- 20
- 0.20
+ 0.25
- 0.80
> Relative frequency = count ÷ total = $20/80 = 0.25$, or 25%.

### What is a Pareto chart?
@ bar-charts
- A pie chart with more than five slices
- A histogram with touching bars
+ A bar chart with the bars arranged in decreasing order of frequency
- A bar chart that uses relative frequencies
> Sorting the bars from tallest to shortest makes it easy to see which categories dominate.

### School A has 200 students and School B has 400. Why use a relative frequency bar chart to compare participation in clubs?
@ bar-charts
- Relative frequencies are easier to calculate
+ The schools are different sizes, so proportions put them on the same scale
- Raw counts can't be shown in a bar chart
- Relative frequency bar charts don't need labels
> The bigger school could have more participants just because it has more students. Proportions compare the rates fairly.

### In a pie chart, a category makes up 25% of the data. What is the angle of its slice?
@ pie-charts
- 25°
- 45°
- 180°
+ 90°
> Angle = relative frequency × 360° = $0.25 \times 360° = 90°$.

### In a class of 40 students, 10 prefer rock music. What is the angle of rock's slice in a pie chart?
@ pie-charts
- 10°
+ 90°
- 40°
- 36°
> $10/40 = 0.25$, and $0.25 \times 360° = 90°$.

### What kind of relationship are pie charts best at showing?
@ pie-charts
+ Part-to-whole: what share of the total each category makes up
- How two numerical variables are related
- How a variable changes over time
- The spread of a numerical variable
> The whole circle represents 100% of the data, and each slice shows one category's share.

### Why do statisticians often prefer bar charts over pie charts?
@ pie-charts
- Bar charts can show more colors
+ People compare bar lengths more accurately than slice angles or areas
- Pie charts can't show percentages
- Bar charts work for numerical data and pie charts don't
> When several categories have similar sizes, slices are hard to compare, while bar heights are easy to read.

### When can a pie chart be a reasonable choice?
@ pie-charts
- When there are 15 categories of similar size
- When the data are numerical
+ When there are only two or three categories with clearly different shares of a whole
- When the categories don't add up to a whole
> With few, clearly different categories that make up a whole, a pie chart communicates the shares well.
