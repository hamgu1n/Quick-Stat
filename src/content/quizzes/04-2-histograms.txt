### What type of data does a histogram display?
@ section-2-6-histograms
- Categorical data
+ Numerical data
- Two numerical variables at once
- Only percentages
> Histograms show the distribution of a numerical (quantitative) variable.

### What are the intervals that a histogram groups values into called?
@ what-is-a-histogram
- Strata
- Quartiles
+ Bins
- Categories
> A histogram divides the range of the data into equal-width bins and draws a bar for each.

### Why do the bars in a histogram touch?
@ what-is-a-histogram
- To make the chart look less cluttered
- Because each bar represents a different category
+ The variable is numerical and continuous, with no gaps between possible values
- Because the bins have different widths
> Touching bars show a continuous number line. Bar charts leave gaps because their categories are separate.

### A histogram of 50 values uses only 2 bins. What is the likely problem?
@ what-is-a-histogram
+ It will hide the shape of the distribution
- It will look jagged and noisy
- It will show too many outliers
- Nothing; fewer bins are always better
> Too few bins lump the data into a featureless block. Too many bins make the histogram jagged and noisy.

### A histogram has a long tail stretching to the right. How is it described?
@ describing-the-shape-of-a-distribution
- Skewed left
+ Skewed right
- Symmetric
- Bimodal
> The skew is named after the side with the long tail.

### Scores on a very easy quiz pile up near 10 out of 10, with a few low scores. What shape is the histogram?
@ describing-the-shape-of-a-distribution

- Symmetric

### Which is a classic example of a right-skewed distribution?
@ describing-the-shape-of-a-distribution
- Adult heights
- Scores on a very easy quiz
- Coin flips
+ Household incomes
> Most people earn moderate incomes, but a few earn very high incomes, stretching the right tail.

### A histogram of restaurant tips is strongly skewed right. Which is likely larger?
@ describing-the-shape-of-a-distribution
- The median
+ The mean
- They will be exactly equal
- There's no way to tell
> A few large tips pull the mean toward the right tail, while the median stays with the bulk of the data.

### A histogram of heights for a mixed group of children and adults shows two clear peaks. What is this called, and what might it suggest?
@ describing-the-shape-of-a-distribution
- Unimodal; the data are normal
- Skewed; there is an outlier
+ Bimodal; the data may come from two distinct groups
- Symmetric; the mean equals the median
> Two peaks often mean two subgroups are mixed together, here children and adults.
