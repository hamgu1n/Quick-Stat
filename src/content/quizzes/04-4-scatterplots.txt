Section Review: Scatterplots. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### What does a scatterplot display?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- The distribution of one numerical variable
+ The relationship between two numerical variables
- How a categorical variable splits into groups
- The five-number summary
> Each dot is one observation, plotted using its values for both variables.

### A study looks at whether hours of exercise predict resting heart rate. Which variable goes on the x-axis?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- Resting heart rate (the response variable)
- It doesn't matter at all
+ Hours of exercise (the explanatory variable)
- Whichever variable has larger numbers
> The explanatory variable goes on the x-axis and the response variable on the y-axis.

### What three features describe the relationship in a scatterplot?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- Mean, median, and mode
- Center, spread, and skew
+ Direction, form, and strength
- Minimum, maximum, and range
> Direction (positive or negative), form (linear or curved), and strength (how tightly the points follow the trend).

### As outdoor temperature rises, hot coffee sales tend to fall. What type of association is this?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- Positive
- No association
- Curved
+ Negative
> When one variable goes up and the other tends to go down, the association is negative.

### Which pair of variables would most likely show a positive association?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
+ Height and weight of adults
- Number of absences and final grade
- Hours of sleep and data-entry errors
- Shoe size and favorite color
> Taller adults tend to weigh more, so both variables move in the same direction.

### The points in a scatterplot lie very close to a straight line that rises from left to right. How would you describe it?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- Weak, negative, linear
+ Strong, positive, linear
- Strong, negative, curved
- Weak, positive, curved
> Rising means positive, a straight-line pattern means linear, and points close to the line mean strong.

### What is an outlier in a scatterplot?
@ outliers-in-scatterplots
+ A point that falls far from the overall trend
- Any point with a large y-value
- The point with the smallest x-value
- A point that lies exactly on the trend line
> A point can be unusual in its x-value, its y-value, or both, relative to the overall pattern.

### Why are some scatterplot outliers called influential points?
@ outliers-in-scatterplots
- They always come from data-entry errors
- They are always removed before analysis
- They have the largest y-values
+ They can dramatically shift a line fitted through the data
> Points far from the trend, especially at the edges of the x-range, can pull a fitted line toward them.

### What should you do when you find an outlier in a scatterplot?
@ outliers-in-scatterplots
- Always delete it
- Always keep it without comment
+ Investigate it: it could be an error or an important genuine observation
- Replace it with the average point
> Outliers shouldn't be removed automatically. Some are mistakes; others are scientifically important.

### Ice cream sales and drownings show a strong positive association across months. What's the best explanation?
@ what-is-a-scatterplot
- Eating ice cream causes drowning
+ A lurking variable, hot weather, increases both
- Drowning causes people to buy ice cream
- The association must be a mistake
> Association does not imply causation. In hot weather, people buy more ice cream and swim more.
