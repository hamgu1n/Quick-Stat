### The probability it rains tomorrow is 0.35. What is the probability it doesn't rain?
@ complement-union-and-intersection
+ 0.65
- 0.35
- 1.35
- 0.5
> Complement rule: $P(A^c) = 1 - P(A) = 1 - 0.35 = 0.65$.

### At a fair, 50% of visitors ride the Ferris wheel, 40% ride the roller coaster, and 15% ride both. What is the probability a visitor rides at least one?
@ complement-union-and-intersection
+ 0.75
- 0.90
- 0.65
- 0.20
> Addition rule: $0.50 + 0.40 - 0.15 = 0.75$. We subtract the overlap so it isn't counted twice.

### What does it mean for two events to be mutually exclusive?
@ complement-union-and-intersection
- One event makes the other more likely
+ They cannot both happen at the same time
- They have the same probability
- Knowing one happened doesn't change the probability of the other
> Mutually exclusive (disjoint) events have no overlap: $P(A \cap B) = 0$.

### One card is drawn from a standard deck. What is the probability it is a heart or a club?

> Hearts and clubs are mutually exclusive, so add: $13/52 + 13/52 = 26/52 = 0.5$.

### $P(A) = 0.4$ and $P(B) = 0.5$. If $A$ and $B$ are independent, what is $P(A \cap B)$?

+ 0.2
- 0.9

### $P(A) = 0.3$, $P(B) = 0.6$, and the actual $P(A \cap B) = 0.25$. Are $A$ and $B$ independent?
@ independent-events
- Yes, because both probabilities are less than 1
- Yes, because $0.3 + 0.6 < 1$
- No, because they are mutually exclusive
+ No, because $0.3 \times 0.6 = 0.18$, which is not 0.25
> Independent events must satisfy $P(A \cap B) = P(A) \times P(B)$. Here $0.25 \neq 0.18$.
