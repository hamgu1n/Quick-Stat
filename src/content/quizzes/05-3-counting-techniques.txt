> Use permutations when roles or arrangement matter, and combinations when you're just choosing a group.

### A committee of 3 is chosen from 6 people, all with equal status. How many committees are possible?

### How are $P(n, r)$ and $C(n, r)$ related?
@ combinations
- $P(n, r) = C(n, r) + r$
+ $P(n, r) = C(n, r) \times r!$
- $C(n, r) = P(n, r) \times r!$
- They are always equal
> Each group of $r$ can be arranged in $r!$ orders, so permutations equal combinations times $r!$.
