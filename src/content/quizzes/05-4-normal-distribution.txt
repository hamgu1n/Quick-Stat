Section Review: The Normal Distribution. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### Which two parameters completely describe a normal distribution?
@ the-normal-curve
- The median and the IQR
- The minimum and the maximum
+ The mean $\mu$ and the standard deviation $\sigma$
- The mode and the range
> We write $X \sim N(\mu, \sigma)$. The mean sets the center and the standard deviation sets the spread.

### For a normal distribution, how do the mean, median, and mode compare?
@ the-normal-curve
- The mean is largest
+ They are all equal
- The median is largest
- It depends on the standard deviation
> A normal curve is symmetric about its mean, so all three measures of center are the same.

### For a continuous variable like $X \sim N(50, 5)$, how is probability represented?
@ the-normal-curve
- By the height of the curve
- By the number of data points
+ By the area under the curve
- By the z-score
> The probability that $X$ falls between two values is the area under the curve between them. The total area is 1.

### Heights are normal with mean 66 inches and SD 3 inches. About what percentage are between 60 and 72 inches?
@ the-empirical-rule
- 68%
- 99.7%
- 50%
+ 95%
> 60 and 72 are each 2 SDs from the mean, and about 95% of values fall within 2 SDs.

### Using the same heights (mean 66, SD 3), about what percentage of people are taller than 69 inches?
@ the-empirical-rule
- 32%
- 34%
- 2.5%
+ 16%
> 68% fall within 1 SD (63 to 69), so 32% are outside, split evenly: 16% above 69.

### What are the mean and standard deviation of the standard normal distribution?
@ the-standard-normal-distribution
- Mean 1, standard deviation 0
+ Mean 0, standard deviation 1
- Mean 0, standard deviation 0
- Mean 100, standard deviation 15
> The standard normal is $Z \sim N(0, 1)$. Any normal variable can be converted to it with $z = (x - \mu)/\sigma$.

### Test scores are $N(70, 10)$. What is the z-score for a score of 85?
@ the-standard-normal-distribution
- 0.85
+ 1.5
- 15
- −1.5
> $z = (85 - 70)/10 = 1.5$.

### A z-table shows $P(Z < 1.5) = 0.9332$. What is $P(Z > 1.5)$?
@ finding-probabilities
- 0.9332
- 0.4332
+ 0.0668
- 1.5
> Z-tables give left areas, so use the complement rule: $1 - 0.9332 = 0.0668$.

### Scores are $N(70, 10)$. The 90th percentile has $z \approx 1.28$. What score is the 90th percentile?
@ finding-percentiles
+ 82.8
- 71.28
- 57.2
- 90
> $x = \mu + z\sigma = 70 + 1.28(10) = 82.8$.

### How can you check whether a normal model is reasonable for a dataset?
@ is-the-normal-model-appropriate
- Check that the mean is larger than the median
- Make sure the sample size is exactly 30
- Check that there are no repeated values
+ Look for a roughly symmetric, bell-shaped histogram and a straight-line normal probability plot
> If the histogram is skewed or the Q-Q plot bends, a normal model may not fit.
