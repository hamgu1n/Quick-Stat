### What is a Bernoulli trial?
@ bernoulli-trials
- A trial with exactly three outcomes
+ A random experiment with exactly two outcomes, success and failure
- A trial that always succeeds
- A set of $n$ repeated experiments
> The probability of success is $p$ and the probability of failure is $1 - p$.

### In a study of returned orders, a returned order is labeled a "success." Why?
@ bernoulli-trials
- Returns are good for the business
- The label is a mistake
+ "Success" just means the outcome we are counting
- Returns happen more than half the time
> "Success" doesn't have to mean something good.

### Which is NOT one of the four conditions for a binomial setting?
@ the-binomial-setting
- A fixed number of trials
+ A sample size of at least 30
- Independent trials
- The same probability of success on every trial
> The four conditions are: fixed $n$, two outcomes, independent trials, and the same $p$.

### You keep flipping a coin until you get heads. $X$ is the number of flips. Why isn't $X$ binomial?
@ the-binomial-setting
- The coin has more than two outcomes
+ There is no fixed number of trials
- The flips aren't independent
- The probability of heads changes
> A binomial variable needs a fixed number of trials, decided in advance.

### You draw 5 cards without replacement and count hearts. Why isn't this binomial?
@ the-binomial-setting
- There are more than two outcomes per draw
- The number of draws isn't fixed
+ The probability of a heart changes after each draw, so the trials aren't independent
- It is binomial
> Without replacement, each draw changes what's left in the deck.

### A fair coin is flipped 4 times. What is the probability of exactly 2 heads?
@ the-binomial-formula
- 0.5
- 0.25
- 0.0625
+ 0.375
> $\binom{4}{2}(0.5)^2(0.5)^2 = 6 \times 0.0625 = 0.375$.

### In the binomial formula $P(X = k) = \binom{n}{k} p^k (1-p)^{n-k}$, what does $\binom{n}{k}$ count?
@ the-binomial-formula
+ The number of ways to arrange $k$ successes among $n$ trials
- The probability of $k$ successes in a row
- The expected number of successes
- The number of failures
> Each arrangement has the same probability, $p^k(1-p)^{n-k}$, so we multiply by the number of arrangements.

### Each of 5 independent trials has $p = 0.2$. What is the probability of at least one success?
@ the-binomial-formula
- 0.2
- 0.32768
+ 0.67232
- 1
> $P(X = 0) = (0.8)^5 = 0.32768$, so $P(X \geq 1) = 1 - 0.32768 = 0.67232$.

### $X \sim \text{Binomial}(50, 0.3)$. What are the mean and standard deviation?
@ mean-and-standard-deviation-of-a-binomial
- $\mu = 15$, $\sigma = 10.5$
- $\mu = 35$, $\sigma \approx 3.24$
- $\mu = 0.3$, $\sigma = 50$
+ $\mu = 15$, $\sigma \approx 3.24$
> $\mu = np = 15$ and $\sigma = \sqrt{np(1-p)} = \sqrt{10.5} \approx 3.24$.

### When is the normal approximation to a binomial reasonable?
@ the-normal-approximation-to-the-binomial
+ When $np \geq 10$ and $n(1-p) \geq 10$
- When $n \geq 30$
- When $p = 0.5$ exactly
- Always
> This success-failure condition means we expect at least 10 successes and at least 10 failures.
