### What is the difference between a parameter and a statistic?
@ parameters-vs-statistics
- A parameter is calculated from a sample; a statistic describes a population
+ A parameter describes a population; a statistic is calculated from a sample
- A parameter changes from sample to sample; a statistic is fixed
- They mean the same thing
> Parameters are fixed but usually unknown. Statistics vary from sample to sample and are used to estimate parameters.

### A poll of 1,000 voters finds 52% support a candidate. Is 52% a parameter or a statistic?
@ parameters-vs-statistics
+ Statistic
- Parameter
- Neither
- Both
> It was calculated from a sample of 1,000 voters, so it's a statistic.

### The true proportion of all defective chips a factory made this month is a:
@ parameters-vs-statistics
- Statistic
+ Parameter
- Sampling distribution
- Standard error
> It describes everything the factory produced, the whole population.

### What is a sampling distribution of $\bar{x}$?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
- The distribution of the values in one sample
- The distribution of the population
+ The distribution of sample means across all possible samples of size $n$
- A list of every possible sample size
> If we took sample after sample and recorded each mean, those means would form the sampling distribution.

### What is the mean of the sampling distribution of $\bar{x}$?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
+ The population mean $\mu$
- $\sigma / \sqrt{n}$
- Always 0
- The mean of the first sample taken
> $\mu_{\bar{x}} = \mu$, which is why $\bar{x}$ is an unbiased estimator of $\mu$.

### What is the standard deviation of the sampling distribution of $\bar{x}$ called?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
- The variance
+ The standard error
- The margin of error
- The z-score
> The standard error is $\sigma_{\bar{x}} = \sigma / \sqrt{n}$.

### A population has $\sigma = 20$. What is the standard error of $\bar{x}$ for samples of size $n = 25$?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
- 20
- 0.8
- 5
+ 4
> $\sigma / \sqrt{n} = 20 / \sqrt{25} = 20 / 5 = 4$.

### What happens to the standard error as the sample size increases?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
+ It decreases
- It increases
- It stays the same
- It becomes equal to $\sigma$
> Larger samples give sample means that cluster more tightly around $\mu$.

### If the sample size is multiplied by 4, what happens to the standard error?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
- It is divided by 4
- It doubles
- It stays the same
+ It is cut in half
> The standard error depends on $\sqrt{n}$, and $\sqrt{4} = 2$.

### Bulbs last $\mu = 1{,}000$ hours with $\sigma = 80$. For a sample of 25 bulbs, the standard error is 16 hours. Would a sample mean of 940 hours be surprising?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-the-mean
- No, it's within normal sampling variation
- No, because individual bulbs vary by 80 hours
+ Yes, it is 3.75 standard errors below the mean
- We can't tell without the sample data
> $(940 - 1000)/16 = -3.75$. A sample mean that far from $\mu$ would be very unusual.
