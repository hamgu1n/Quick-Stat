Section Review: Point Estimates. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### What is a point estimate?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- A range of plausible values for a parameter
- The true value of a population parameter
+ A single value from a sample used as our best guess for a population parameter
- The margin of error of a survey
> It's called a "point" estimate because it gives one number rather than a range.

### Which sample statistic estimates the population mean $\mu$?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- $\hat{p}$
- $s$
- $\sigma$
+ $\bar{x}$
> The sample mean $\bar{x}$ is the point estimate of $\mu$.

### Which symbol is the point estimate of a population proportion $p$?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- $\bar{x}$
+ $\hat{p}$
- $s$
- $\mu$
> The sample proportion $\hat{p}$ ("p-hat") estimates $p$.

### In a survey of 250 students, 150 prefer morning classes. What is $\hat{p}$?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
+ 0.60
- 0.40
- 150
- 1.67
> $\hat{p} = 150/250 = 0.60$.

### A survey of 50 employees finds the standard deviation of weekly hours is 6.2. What parameter does 6.2 estimate?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- The population mean $\mu$
- The sample mean $\bar{x}$
+ The population standard deviation $\sigma$
- The population proportion $p$
> The sample standard deviation $s = 6.2$ is a point estimate of $\sigma$.

### What does it mean for an estimator to be unbiased?
@ properties-of-good-estimators
- It always equals the parameter exactly
+ On average across all possible samples, it equals the true parameter
- It has no variability from sample to sample
- It was calculated from a random sample
> An unbiased estimator hits the right target on average, even though any one sample may miss.

### Why do we divide by $n - 1$ instead of $n$ when computing the sample variance $s^2$?
@ properties-of-good-estimators
+ It makes $s^2$ an unbiased estimator of $\sigma^2$
- It makes the calculation easier
- It removes outliers from the data
- It makes the variance larger than the standard deviation
> Dividing by $n$ would underestimate $\sigma^2$ on average.

### Two different surveys of 500 students give $\hat{p} = 0.64$ and $\hat{p} = 0.61$. What is the best explanation?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- One survey must have made a mistake
+ Sampling variability: different samples give slightly different estimates
- The population proportion changed between surveys
- Point estimates are always wrong
> Different samples contain different people, so the statistic varies from sample to sample.

### What is the main limitation of a point estimate on its own?
@ properties-of-good-estimators
- It is always biased
- It can't be calculated from a sample
- It only works for proportions
+ It gives no indication of how accurate it is
> That's why we pair it with a margin of error or confidence interval.

### Daily calorie counts for 4 adults are 1,800, 2,000, 2,100, and 2,300. What is the point estimate of the population mean?
@ what-is-a-point-estimate
- 2,100
- 2,000
- 8,200
+ 2,050
> $\bar{x} = (1{,}800 + 2{,}000 + 2{,}100 + 2{,}300)/4 = 8{,}200/4 = 2{,}050$.
