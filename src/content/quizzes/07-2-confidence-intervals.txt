Section Review: Confidence Intervals for Means and Proportions. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### What is the general form of a confidence interval?
@ what-is-a-confidence-interval
+ Point estimate ± Margin of error
- Parameter ± Standard deviation
- Sample size ± Critical value
- Mean ± Median
> The margin of error shows how far the estimate might reasonably be from the true parameter.

### What does "95% confident" mean for a confidence interval?
@ what-is-a-confidence-interval
- There is a 95% probability that the parameter is in this particular interval
- 95% of the data fall inside the interval
+ If we repeated the sampling many times, about 95% of the intervals would contain the true parameter
- The sample mean is correct 95% of the time
> The parameter is fixed; the interval is what varies. The 95% describes the method.

### What is the $z^*$ critical value for a 95% confidence interval?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-known-sigma
+ 1.960
- 1.645
- 2.576
- 0.95
> 90% uses 1.645, 95% uses 1.960, and 99% uses 2.576.

### A sample of 25 has $\bar{x} = 50$, and $\sigma = 10$ is known. What is the 95% confidence interval for $\mu$?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-known-sigma
- $50 \pm 19.6$
- $50 \pm 1.96$
- $50 \pm 10$
+ $50 \pm 3.92$
> $\sigma/\sqrt{n} = 10/5 = 2$, so the margin of error is $1.96 \times 2 = 3.92$: (46.08, 53.92).

### Raising the confidence level from 95% to 99% (with everything else the same) makes the interval:
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-known-sigma
- Narrower
- The same width
- Centered at a different value
+ Wider
> A higher confidence level uses a larger critical value (2.576 instead of 1.96).

### When should you use a t-interval instead of a z-interval for a mean?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-unknown-sigma
- When the sample size is larger than 30
- When estimating a proportion
- When the data are categorical
+ When the population standard deviation $\sigma$ is unknown and estimated with $s$
> In practice $\sigma$ is almost never known, so t-intervals are the usual choice.

### How does the t-distribution compare to the standard normal distribution?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-unknown-sigma
- It is skewed right
- It has thinner tails
+ It is bell-shaped and symmetric but has heavier tails
- It is identical for every sample size
> The heavier tails reflect the extra uncertainty from estimating $\sigma$. As $n$ grows, t approaches the normal.

### In a survey of 300 people, 120 say yes. What is the 95% confidence interval for $p$?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-proportion
- $0.40 \pm 0.028$
- $0.40 \pm 0.096$
+ $0.40 \pm 0.055$
- $0.60 \pm 0.055$
> $\hat{p} = 0.40$ and $SE = \sqrt{0.4(0.6)/300} \approx 0.0283$. The margin of error is $1.96 \times 0.0283 \approx 0.055$.

### What conditions are needed to use the confidence interval for a proportion?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-proportion
- $n \geq 30$
+ $n\hat{p} \geq 10$ and $n(1 - \hat{p}) \geq 10$
- $\hat{p} = 0.5$
- The population must be normal
> We need at least 10 successes and 10 failures in the sample.

### A researcher wants a margin of error of 2 at 95% confidence, with $\sigma = 10$. What is the minimum sample size?
@ confidence-interval-for-a-population-mean-known-sigma
+ 97
- 96
- 10
- 50
> $n = (1.96 \times 10 / 2)^2 = 96.04$. Always round up, so $n = 97$.
