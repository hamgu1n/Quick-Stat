### In an A/B test, visitors are randomly assigned to version A or B. Why does that matter?
@ a-b-testing
+ It makes the test a randomized experiment, so a significant difference supports cause and effect
- It makes the sample larger
- It guarantees version B will win
- It removes the need for a hypothesis test
> Random assignment balances other variables, so a difference can be credited to the version.

### What is the point estimate of $p_1 - p_2$?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-hat-p-1-hat-p-2
- $p_1 - p_2$
- $\hat{p}_1 + \hat{p}_2$
- $\hat{p}_{pool}$
+ $\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2$
> We estimate the difference in population proportions with the difference in sample proportions.

### In the standard error of $\hat{p}_1 - \hat{p}_2$, what happens to the two groups' variances?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-hat-p-1-hat-p-2
- They subtract
- They multiply
+ They add
- Only the larger one is used
> Each sample adds its own uncertainty, so the difference is more uncertain than either proportion alone.

### Which is NOT a condition for inference about $p_1 - p_2$?
@ the-sampling-distribution-of-hat-p-1-hat-p-2
- Observations are independent within each group
- The two groups are independent of each other
- At least 10 successes and 10 failures in each group
+ The two groups must have the same sample size
> The groups can be different sizes. They just need to be independent and large enough.

### A 95% confidence interval for $p_1 - p_2$ is (0.251, 0.349). What does it suggest?
@ confidence-interval-for-p-1-p-2
+ $p_1$ is larger than $p_2$, since the whole interval is above 0
- There is no difference between the groups
- $p_2$ is larger than $p_1$
- The interval is too wide to say anything
> If the interval doesn't contain 0, "no difference" isn't plausible.

### A 95% confidence interval for $p_1 - p_2$ is $(-0.03, 0.06)$. What can you conclude?
@ confidence-interval-for-p-1-p-2
- $p_1$ is definitely larger
- $p_2$ is definitely larger
+ "No difference" is plausible, since the interval contains 0
- The groups are identical
> An interval containing 0 means the data are consistent with no difference.

### Group 1 has 30 successes out of 100, and group 2 has 50 out of 150. What is the pooled proportion?
@ hypothesis-test-for-p-1-p-2
- 0.317
+ 0.32
- 0.633
- 0.30
> $\hat{p}_{pool} = (30 + 50)/(100 + 150) = 80/250 = 0.32$.

### When do you use the pooled proportion?
@ hypothesis-test-for-p-1-p-2
- When building a confidence interval for $p_1 - p_2$
- Whenever the sample sizes differ
+ When testing $H_0: p_1 = p_2$, because the test assumes the proportions are equal
- Never; it's only for means
> A confidence interval doesn't assume equal proportions, so it uses $\hat{p}_1$ and $\hat{p}_2$ separately.

### What are the hypotheses for testing whether two proportions differ?
@ hypothesis-test-for-p-1-p-2
+ $H_0: p_1 = p_2$ vs. $H_a: p_1 \neq p_2$
- $H_0: \hat{p}_1 = \hat{p}_2$ vs. $H_a: \hat{p}_1 \neq \hat{p}_2$
- $H_0: p_1 \neq p_2$ vs. $H_a: p_1 = p_2$
- $H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 1$ vs. $H_a: p_1 - p_2 \neq 1$
> $H_0: p_1 = p_2$ can also be written $H_0: p_1 - p_2 = 0$.

### A researcher finds photo posts get 100+ likes significantly more often than posts without photos. The posters chose whether to add photos. What can they conclude?
