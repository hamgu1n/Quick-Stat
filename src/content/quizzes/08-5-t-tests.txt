### When should you use a t-test instead of a z-test for a mean?
@ section-4-7-t-tests
- When the sample size is larger than 30
- When the data are categorical
- When comparing three or more groups
+ When the population standard deviation $\sigma$ is unknown and estimated with $s$
> Estimating $\sigma$ with $s$ adds uncertainty, which the t-distribution accounts for.

### How does the t-distribution compare to the standard normal?
@ the-t-distribution
+ It is symmetric and bell-shaped but has heavier tails
- It is skewed right
- It has thinner tails
- It can't take negative values
> The heavier tails reflect the extra variability from using $s$.

### What happens to the t-distribution as the degrees of freedom increase?
@ the-t-distribution
- Its tails get heavier
- It becomes skewed
+ It gets closer to the standard normal
- It stops being symmetric
> By about $df = 30$, the t and standard normal are nearly identical.

### A sample of 25 has $\bar{x} = 105$ and $s = 10$. Test $H_0: \mu = 100$. What is the t-statistic?
@ one-sample-t-test
- 0.5
+ 2.5
- 5
- 12.5
> $t = (105 - 100)/(10/\sqrt{25}) = 5/2 = 2.5$.

### How many degrees of freedom does a one-sample t-test with $n = 25$ have?
@ one-sample-t-test
+ 24
- 25
- 26
- 23
> $df = n - 1 = 24$.

### What does a two-sample t-test compare?
@ two-sample-t-test
- One sample mean to a fixed value
- Before-and-after measurements on the same people
- The proportions of two groups
+ The means of two independent groups
> Its hypotheses are $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2$ vs. $H_a: \mu_1 \neq \mu_2$ (or $<$, $>$).

### Researchers measure blood pressure on the same patients before and after an exercise program. Which test fits?
@ paired-t-test
- Two-sample t-test
- One-sample z-test
- Chi-square test
+ Paired t-test
> Each "after" value is linked to a "before" value from the same person.

### How is a paired t-test carried out?
@ paired-t-test
- Compare the two group means with the two-sample formula
- Add the two groups together and test the total
+ Compute each pair's difference, then do a one-sample t-test on the differences
- Use a chi-square test on the pairs
> We test $H_0: \mu_d = 0$, where $\mu_d$ is the mean difference.

### A company compares mean salaries at two different firms using separate random samples. Which test fits?
@ two-sample-t-test
- Paired t-test
+ Two-sample t-test
- One-sample t-test
- Goodness-of-fit test
> The samples come from two independent groups.

### A two-sample t-test gives p = 0.059 at $\alpha = 0.05$. Which conclusion is correct?
@ two-sample-t-test
- Reject $H_0$; the means are different
- Accept $H_0$; the means are equal
+ Fail to reject $H_0$; there isn't enough evidence the means differ
- The test is invalid
> $0.059 > 0.05$, so the evidence isn't strong enough. That doesn't prove the means are equal.
