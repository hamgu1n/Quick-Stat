Section Review: One-Way ANOVA. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### Why use ANOVA instead of running several t-tests to compare three groups?
@ section-4-8-one-way-anova
- t-tests can't compare means
- ANOVA always gives smaller p-values
- t-tests only work with paired data
+ Running many tests inflates the chance of at least one Type I error
> Each test has its own 5% false-positive risk, so the overall risk grows with every test.

### What are the hypotheses for a one-way ANOVA with three groups?
@ the-anova-idea
- $H_0$: all means are different vs. $H_a$: all means are equal
- $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3$ vs. $H_a$: all three means are different
+ $H_0: \mu_1 = \mu_2 = \mu_3$ vs. $H_a$: at least one mean is different
- $H_0: \mu_1 > \mu_2 > \mu_3$ vs. $H_a$: they are equal
> The alternative is "at least one differs," not "all differ."

### ANOVA splits total variability into which two parts?
@ the-anova-idea
- Variability before and after treatment
+ Variability between groups and variability within groups
- Variance and standard deviation
- Sample size and significance level
> If the between-group variability is large compared to the within-group variability, the means likely differ.

### What is the F-statistic?
@ the-f-statistic
+ MSB / MSW
- MSW / MSB
- SSB + SSW
- $k - 1$
> F compares between-group to within-group variability.

### An ANOVA compares 4 groups with 40 observations in total. What are the degrees of freedom?
@ the-f-statistic
- $df_1 = 4$, $df_2 = 40$
+ $df_1 = 3$, $df_2 = 36$
- $df_1 = 3$, $df_2 = 39$
- $df_1 = 39$, $df_2 = 3$
> $df_1 = k - 1 = 3$ and $df_2 = N - k = 36$.

### With 3 groups and 33 observations, SSB = 60 and SSW = 180. What is F?
@ the-f-statistic
- 0.33
- 3
- 10
+ 5
> MSB $= 60/2 = 30$ and MSW $= 180/30 = 6$, so $F = 30/6 = 5$.

### What does a large F-statistic suggest?
@ the-f-statistic
+ Between-group variation dominates, so at least one mean likely differs
- The groups have equal means
- The within-group variability is large
- The sample is too small
> A large F leads to a small p-value and rejecting $H_0$.

### Which is NOT an assumption of one-way ANOVA?
@ assumptions-of-one-way-anova
+ Equal sample sizes in every group
- Independent observations
- Normal data within each group
- Equal population variances
> The groups don't need the same sizes.

### Three groups have sample standard deviations of 3, 4, and 7. Is the equal-variance rule of thumb met?
@ assumptions-of-one-way-anova
- Yes, all are under 10
+ No, the largest is more than twice the smallest
- Yes, the middle one is about the average
- We need the means to decide
> $7 / 3 \approx 2.3$, which is greater than 2.

### An ANOVA rejects $H_0$. What do you know?
@ the-anova-idea
- All group means are different
- The first group has the largest mean
+ At least one group mean differs, but not which one
- The assumptions were violated
> Post-hoc tests are needed to find which means differ.
