Section Review: Interpreting ANOVA Results. Format notes are in src/lib/quiz.ts.

### An ANOVA comparing four groups has p = 0.003. What does that tell you?
@ section-4-9-interpreting-anova-results
- All four group means are different
- The first and last groups differ
- None of the means differ
+ At least one group mean differs from the others
> A significant F-test shows that some difference exists, but not where.

### What does a significant ANOVA result NOT tell you?
@ section-4-9-interpreting-anova-results
- That at least one mean differs
- That the p-value is below $\alpha$
+ Which specific groups differ from each other
- That the null hypothesis was rejected
> Post-hoc tests are needed to identify which pairs of means differ.

### How many pairwise comparisons are there among 5 groups?
@ the-problem-of-multiple-comparisons
- 5
- 20
- 25
+ 10
> $\binom{5}{2} = 10$.

### Why not just run a t-test for every pair of groups after ANOVA?
@ the-problem-of-multiple-comparisons
- t-tests can't compare two groups
- It would take too long to compute
+ Running many tests inflates the overall Type I error rate
- t-tests always disagree with ANOVA
> With more comparisons, a false positive somewhere becomes increasingly likely.

### What is a post-hoc test?
@ the-problem-of-multiple-comparisons
- A test run before collecting data
+ A follow-up test that compares groups while controlling the overall error rate
- A test that replaces ANOVA entirely
- A test for categorical data
> Tukey's HSD and the Bonferroni correction are common post-hoc methods.

### Which post-hoc test is most commonly used when group sizes are equal or similar?
@ the-problem-of-multiple-comparisons
+ Tukey's Honestly Significant Difference (HSD)
- The chi-square test
- The paired t-test
- The sign test
> Tukey's HSD compares all pairs while holding the family-wise error rate at $\alpha$.

### With $\alpha = 0.05$ and 5 comparisons, what significance level does the Bonferroni correction use for each test?
@ the-problem-of-multiple-comparisons
- 0.05
- 0.25
- 0.001
+ 0.01
> Bonferroni divides $\alpha$ by the number of comparisons: $0.05 / 5 = 0.01$.

### An ANOVA table has MS Between = 42.2 and MS Within = 6.2. What is F?
@ reading-the-anova-output
+ About 6.8
- 36.0
- 0.15
- 48.4
> $F = \text{MSB}/\text{MSW} = 42.2/6.2 \approx 6.8$.

### In an ANOVA table, the within-group SS is much larger than the between-group SS, but F is still significant. How is that possible?
@ reading-the-anova-output
- The table must have an error
+ F compares mean squares (SS divided by df), and the between-group SS has far fewer degrees of freedom
- F only uses the between-group SS
- A larger within-group SS always gives a larger F
> Dividing each SS by its df can make the between-group mean square much larger.

### ANOVA is significant, and Tukey finds only Groups 1 and 2 differ. Which summary is correct?
@ reading-the-anova-output
- All three groups differ from one another
+ Groups 1 and 2 have significantly different means; no other pairs differ significantly
- Group 3 is different from everyone
- The ANOVA result was a false positive
> Report the post-hoc results alongside the overall ANOVA so readers know which groups differ.
