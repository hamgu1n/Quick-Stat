### What makes a test nonparametric?
@ the-idea-behind-nonparametric-tests
- It uses a larger sample
+ It makes few or no assumptions about the population's distribution
- It only works for normal data
- It always gives smaller p-values
> Parametric tests usually assume normality; nonparametric tests don't.

### Why are rank-based tests robust to outliers?
@ the-idea-behind-nonparametric-tests
- They delete outliers before testing
+ A value's rank is the same no matter how extreme it is
- They use the mean instead of the median
- Outliers always get rank 1
> Whether the largest value is 50 or 50,000, it gets the top rank.

### What are the ranks of the values 12, 4, 20, 9?
@ the-idea-behind-nonparametric-tests
- 1, 2, 3, 4
- 2, 4, 1, 3
- 12, 4, 20, 9
+ 3, 1, 4, 2
> Rank from smallest to largest: 4 gets 1, 9 gets 2, 12 gets 3, and 20 gets 4.

### What does the sign test use from each observation?
@ the-sign-test
- Its exact value
- Its rank among all values
+ Only whether it is above or below the claimed median $M_0$
- Its z-score
> That's why it needs almost no assumptions, but also why it throws away information.

### Under $H_0$, the number of "+" signs in a sign test with 12 values follows which distribution?
@ the-sign-test
- Normal(12, 0.5)
- t with 11 degrees of freedom
+ Binomial(12, 0.5)
- Chi-square with 11 degrees of freedom
> If the median is $M_0$, each value is equally likely to fall above or below it.

### Which test is the nonparametric alternative to the paired t-test?
@ the-wilcoxon-signed-rank-test
+ Wilcoxon signed-rank test
- Mann-Whitney U test
- Kruskal-Wallis test
- Chi-square test
> It ranks the absolute differences and compares the positive and negative rank sums.

### Which test is the nonparametric alternative to the two-sample t-test?
@ the-mann-whitney-u-test
- Wilcoxon signed-rank test
- Sign test
+ Mann-Whitney U test (Wilcoxon rank-sum)
- Kruskal-Wallis test
> It ranks both groups together and compares the rank sums.

### In a Mann-Whitney test, one group holds almost all the high ranks. What does that suggest?
@ the-mann-whitney-u-test
- The groups come from the same distribution
+ That group's values tend to be larger
- The test was done incorrectly
- That group has more outliers
> If the groups were the same, high and low ranks would be mixed evenly.

### Customer ratings on a 1–5 scale are compared across three stores. Which test fits best?
@ the-kruskal-wallis-test
- One-way ANOVA
- Mann-Whitney U test
- Paired t-test
+ Kruskal-Wallis test
> Three independent groups with ordinal data call for Kruskal-Wallis.

### When the conditions for a t-test are met, why is the t-test usually preferred over a nonparametric test?
@ when-should-you-use-a-nonparametric-test
+ The t-test is usually more powerful, so it's more likely to detect a real difference
- Nonparametric tests are always wrong
- The t-test doesn't need any data
- Nonparametric tests can't give p-values
> Choose nonparametric tests for small, skewed, or ordinal data.
