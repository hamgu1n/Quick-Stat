### In $\hat{y} = b_0 + b_1 x$, what does $b_1$ represent?
@ the-simple-linear-regression-model
+ The predicted change in $y$ for a one-unit increase in $x$
- The predicted value of $y$ when $x = 0$
- The correlation between $x$ and $y$
- The residual
> $b_1$ is the slope of the line.

### Why does $\hat{y}$ have a "hat"?
@ the-simple-linear-regression-model
+ It's a predicted (average) value, not an exact observation
- It's the observed value
- It's always larger than $y$
- It means the value is an outlier
> Individual observations vary around the line.

### What is a residual?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- $\hat{y} - y$
+ $y - \hat{y}$, the observed value minus the predicted value
- The slope of the line
- The distance from a point to the y-axis
> $e_i = y_i - \hat{y}_i$.

### What does the least squares line minimize?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- The sum of the residuals
- The correlation
- The number of points above the line
+ The sum of the squared residuals
> Among all straight lines, it has the smallest $\sum e_i^2$.

### Given $r = 0.6$, $s_x = 5$, and $s_y = 10$, what is the slope?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- 0.3
+ 1.2
- 0.6
- 3
> $b_1 = r \cdot s_y / s_x = 0.6 \times 10 / 5 = 1.2$.

### With $\bar{x} = 20$, $\bar{y} = 50$, and slope $b_1 = 1.2$, what is the intercept?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- 50
+ 26
- 24
- 74
> $b_0 = \bar{y} - b_1\bar{x} = 50 - 1.2(20) = 26$.

### Which point does the least squares line always pass through?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- $(0, 0)$
- $(0, \bar{y})$
+ $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$
- The point with the largest residual
> The line always passes through the means of both variables.

### The line is $\hat{y} = 26 + 1.2x$. A point has $x = 25$ and $y = 60$. What is its residual?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- −4
- 56
+ 4
- 60
> $\hat{y} = 26 + 1.2(25) = 56$, so the residual is $60 - 56 = 4$. The point lies above the line.

### A residual is negative. Where is the point relative to the line?
@ finding-the-least-squares-line
- Above the line
- On the line
- At $(\bar{x}, \bar{y})$
+ Below the line
> A negative residual means the observed value is less than predicted.

### A model fit on businesses with 5 to 30 employees is used to predict revenue for one with 150 employees. What's the concern?
@ extrapolation
- The slope will be negative
- The residual will be zero
+ This is extrapolation: we have no evidence the trend continues that far outside the data
- The correlation will change sign
> Predictions far outside the observed range of $x$ are unreliable.
