### What does $R^2$ measure?
@ what-is-r-2
- The slope of the regression line
- The average residual
- Whether $X$ causes $Y$
+ The proportion of the variability in $Y$ explained by the linear relationship with $X$
> $R^2$ compares the model's prediction errors to the variability around $\bar{y}$.

### A model has SST = 500 and SSE = 100. What is $R^2$?
@ what-is-r-2
- 0.2
- 5
+ 0.8
- 400
> $R^2 = 1 - \text{SSE}/\text{SST} = 1 - 100/500 = 0.8$.

### In simple linear regression, $r = 0.9$. What is $R^2$?
@ what-is-r-2
- 0.9
- 0.95
- 0.18
+ 0.81
> $R^2 = r^2 = 0.9^2 = 0.81$.

### $R^2 = 0.64$ and the relationship is negative. What is $r$?
@ what-is-r-2
- 0.64
+ −0.8
- −0.64
- 0.8
> $|r| = \sqrt{0.64} = 0.8$, and the sign matches the direction: $r = -0.8$.

### How should you interpret $R^2 = 0.53$ for advertising spend predicting sales?
@ what-is-r-2
+ About 53% of the variation in sales is explained by the linear relationship with advertising spend
- Advertising causes 53% of sales
- The model is correct 53% of the time
- 53% of the points lie on the line
> The remaining 47% comes from other factors and random variation.

### What does $R^2 = 0$ mean?
@ what-is-r-2
+ The regression line is no better than predicting $\bar{y}$ for everyone
- Every point falls on the line
- The slope is 1
- The model is perfect
> $R^2$ ranges from 0 (explains nothing) to 1 (explains everything).

### A model has a high $R^2$. Which statement is true?
@ r-2-does-not-tell-the-whole-story
- $X$ must cause $Y$
+ The line explains much of the variation, but that alone doesn't prove causation or a good fit
- The model must be the right shape
- The residual plot doesn't need to be checked
> A strong linear relationship can still come from lurking variables or a curved pattern.

### Salary rises steeply from age 25 to 45, then levels off, yet a linear fit gives $R^2 = 0.78$. What's the problem?
@ r-2-does-not-tell-the-whole-story
- There is no problem; 0.78 is high
- $R^2$ should be negative
- The sample must be too small
+ A straight line is the wrong shape and will mispredict systematically
> A residual plot would show a curved pattern, the sign of a poor fit.

### A psychology study reports $R^2 = 0.20$. Does that make the model useless?
@ r-2-does-not-tell-the-whole-story
- Yes, anything below 0.5 is useless
+ Not necessarily: in complex fields, low $R^2$ values are common and the model can still be meaningful
- Yes, because $R^2$ must be at least 0.9
- No, because $R^2$ doesn't matter
> Context, significance, and theory all matter alongside $R^2$.

### What should you examine alongside $R^2$ to judge whether a linear model is appropriate?
@ r-2-does-not-tell-the-whole-story
+ A residual plot
- The sample mean of $x$
- The intercept only
- A pie chart of the residuals
> Residual plots reveal curves and changing spread that $R^2$ hides.
