### What does the acronym LINE stand for in regression?
@ the-assumptions-of-simple-linear-regression
- Least squares, Intercept, Normality, Error
- Linearity, Intercept, No outliers, Extrapolation
+ Linearity, Independence, Normality of residuals, Equal variance
- Leverage, Independence, Normality, Extrapolation
> These are the four main assumptions of simple linear regression.

### Data are collected on the same store every week for two years. Which assumption is most likely violated?
@ the-assumptions-of-simple-linear-regression
+ Independence
- Linearity
- Normality
- Equal variance
> Time series data often violate independence, since nearby weeks tend to be similar.

### What is heteroscedasticity?
@ the-assumptions-of-simple-linear-regression
+ Residual spread that changes systematically across values of $X$
- A curved relationship between $X$ and $Y$
- Residuals that aren't normal
- Observations that aren't independent
> It violates the equal-variance assumption.

### Why does the normality assumption matter less with large samples?
@ the-assumptions-of-simple-linear-regression
- Large samples are always normal
+ The Central Limit Theorem helps the inference procedures work anyway
- Residuals disappear with large samples
- Normality only applies to small samples
> With large samples, confidence intervals and tests for the slope are less sensitive to non-normal residuals.

### What is plotted in a residual plot?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- $x$ versus $y$
- Residuals versus their ranks
- The regression line only
+ Residuals on the y-axis versus fitted values $\hat{y}$ on the x-axis
> It's the main tool for checking linearity and equal variance.

### What does a well-behaved residual plot look like?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- A clear upward trend
- A U-shaped curve
+ Random scatter around zero with roughly constant spread
- A megaphone shape
> Any pattern suggests an assumption is violated.

### A residual plot shows a curve: positive residuals at both ends and negative in the middle. Which assumption fails?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- Independence
- Equal variance
- None
+ Linearity
> The straight line can't follow the true curved relationship.

### A residual plot fans out into a megaphone shape as $\hat{y}$ increases. Which assumption fails?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- Linearity
+ Equal variance
- Independence
- Normality
> Growing spread is heteroscedasticity. A log transformation of $y$ often helps.

### How do you check the normality of residuals?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- With a pie chart
- By computing $R^2$
- By checking the slope's sign
+ With a normal Q-Q plot of the residuals
> If the residuals are normal, the Q-Q plot points fall close to a straight line.

### The residual plot is random, the Q-Q plot is straight, the sample is random, and the residual spread is constant. What do you conclude?
@ checking-assumptions-with-residual-plots
- At least one assumption fails
+ All four LINE assumptions appear satisfied, so the linear model is appropriate
- The model has a high $R^2$
- The relationship is causal
> Each check matches one of the LINE assumptions.
